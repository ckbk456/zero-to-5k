# Evidence and training findings

Research date: 2026-10-16. Public edition: personal context removed. Current time-based product decisions live in `docs/logic/product.md`; distance goals below remain optional research. Status: evidence review and original product proposals, not an individualized exercise prescription.

The initial investigation used two separate subagents, one technical and one health-science researcher. This document consolidates that work and the subsequent progression/guideline investigation. Sources are linked next to the relevant findings. Retrieval limitations are retained explicitly.

## 1. Product population and goal

The intended audience is generally healthy adults working toward three vigorous aerobic sessions weekly, not a diabetes or chronic-heart-disease exercise routine. “Heavy intensity” is interpreted here as vigorous aerobic intensity; it does not mean maximal effort or necessarily high-intensity interval training.

Beginners still need an entry level they tolerate. The eventual vigorous goal must not make every warm-up, recovery, or early technique drill vigorous. Running and race walking are activity choices; endurance, distance, and speed are different goals.

## 3. The remembered 20% rule

An ACSM handout, _Starting a Walking Program_, is a close match. Its indexed text says **“up to 20 percent every two weeks”**, referring to walking time and distance. That is neither a compulsory increase nor a universal rule for all training load. [ACSM handout URL](https://www.acsm.org/docs/default-source/files-for-resource-library/starting-a-walking-program.pdf)

Evidence limitation: the search engine returned the original ACSM-hosted document's indexed text, but direct retrieval on the research date redirected to an HTTP 404. The publication/revision date and continued endorsement were not established. Treat this as an identifiable historical handout, not a freshly verified current ACSM position stand.

No universal sports-society requirement to increase workload by 20% every week was substantiated in this targeted search. This is not proof that no organization has ever used that recommendation.

Arithmetic illustrates the distinction: four successive weekly 20% increases multiply a quantity by 2.0736; two successive fortnightly 20% increases multiply it by 1.44. These calculations are not training prescriptions.

“Workload” needs a denominator: weekly minutes, distance, longest session, longest uninterrupted bout, intensity, and technical-gait exposure are different quantities. A percentage change is also undefined from a zero baseline. Increasing several dimensions together can create a large change despite modest percentages in each.

## 4. What injury research supports—and does not

The GRONORUN randomized trial compared a 13-week novice-running progression using the 10% rule with an eight-week program. It did not find a reduction in running-related injuries from the longer graded program. This does not prove gradual progression is useless or larger increases safe; it limits claims that a specific percentage rule prevents injury. [Buist et al., 2008, DOI 10.1177/0363546507307505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17940147/). The indexed PubMed abstract was available; direct page rendering was incomplete.

A 2025 observational study followed 5,205 runners. Single-session distances more than 10% above the longest run in the preceding 30 days were associated with higher overuse-injury rates; week-to-week ratios were not associated with increased rates in the same way. This was not a randomized intervention, relied on reported injuries, and largely involved experienced runners. It does not establish a universally safe cutoff or validate a race-walking rule. [Frandsen et al., DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2024-109380](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/59/17/1203.long)

Product implication, not a proven injury-prevention intervention: assess both individual sessions and accumulated exposure; do not make one percentage the definition of safety. Keep numerical progression policies separately versioned and subject to professional review.

## 5. Health guidance, NHS goals, and the two-day-gap hypothesis

The AHA currently describes a minimum of 150 moderate or 75 vigorous aerobic minutes weekly, or a combination, preferably distributed across the week. It also recommends strengthening on at least two days and gradual progression. Short activity bouts count. [AHA recommendations, reviewed January 2024](https://www.heart.org/en/healthy-living/exercise-and-physical-activity/fitness-basics/aha-recs-for-physical-activity-in-adults)

Therefore three 25-minute vigorous blocks total 75 minutes; three 30-minute blocks total 90. Count the actual vigorous blocks, not an entire session whose warm-up and recovery are easier. These calculations show compatibility with general guidance, not why the NHS plan was originally designed that way.

The NHS plan describes three sessions weekly and a final 30-minute continuous run. Its overview credits Josh Clark with creating Couch to 5K. No direct source was located establishing that its exact endpoint was derived from ACC/AHA guidance. Calling it a useful health-oriented introduction is a product interpretation; describing it as a documented 150-minute-guideline derivation would overstate the evidence. [NHS plan](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/couch-to-5k-running-plan/), [NHS background](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/)

The historical 2007 ACSM/AHA recommendation did specify 30 minutes of moderate activity on five days, or 20 minutes vigorous on three days. That historical association does not establish the NHS program's design rationale. [Haskell et al., 2007](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/circulationaha.107.185649). Publisher-indexed text was available; direct article access returned 403.

The explicit instruction to avoid more than two consecutive days without activity is present in ADA exercise guidance for diabetes, with a rationale involving transient insulin-sensitivity effects. It must not be relabelled a universal cardiac-health requirement. This clinical pathway is outside the default product scope. [ADA position statement, 2016](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/39/11/2065/37249/Physical-Activity-Exercise-and-Diabetes-A-Position), [joint ACSM/ADA statement, 2010](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/33/12/e147/39268/Exercise-and-Type-2-DiabetesThe-American-College)

## 6. Race walking and knee-related evidence

Race walking is a technique-defined sport, not a synonym for brisk walking: ground contact must be maintained without visible flight, and the advancing leg is straightened from contact until the upright position. Technique demonstrations should be reviewed by a qualified coach; avoid instructions to forcibly lock or hyperextend knees. [World Athletics explanatory resource](https://worldathletics.org/download/downloadnsa?filename=21075f00-0276-41b5-9520-0cbcb8222a1d.pdf&urlslug=race-walking)

A small study of ten competitive racewalkers found mechanical differences including greater external knee hyperextension moments and medial ground-reaction forces. It cannot establish individual clinical suitability or prove comparative injury risk. [Cairns et al., 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3747807/)

An individual reporting a knee limitation may need advice on race-walking suitability. That does not establish a diagnosis, a blanket medical-clearance requirement for all healthy users, or rehabilitation positioning for the app.

Race walking can be vigorous. Use breathing/effort information rather than assuming intensity from the activity label alone. Moderate effort generally permits conversation; vigorous effort limits speech to a few words between breaths. [CDC intensity guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physicalactivity/basics/measuring/index.html)

Sharp persistent pain, limping, significant swelling, or symptoms that do not settle warrant stopping/reducing the aggravating activity and appropriate assessment. Do not encode a universal numerical pain threshold as proof of safety. [CDC archived exercise-pain guidance](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/arthritis/basics/physical-activity/pain.html). This is symptom-context evidence, not the default program's target population.

## 7. Proposed race-walking curriculum

No validated beginner 5 km race-walking protocol for people with knee symptoms was located in the targeted search. The following stages are original design proposals requiring coach and clinical review, not established medical milestones:

1. Establish tolerable ordinary walking if necessary.
2. Learn relaxed race-walking technique with short bouts and easy recovery.
3. Extend technique bouts while holding total session time approximately steady.
4. Build longer bouts and then continuous endurance.
5. Progress toward the selected health-oriented weekly vigorous dose, where appropriate.
6. Optionally develop a measured continuous 5 km and subsequently a time goal.

An earlier illustrative 10–12+ week outline is a planning envelope only. User-selected duration must not turn it into a guaranteed completion date. For a beginner, optional 30–60-second technique bouts and generous easy recovery illustrate the format, not an accepted default prescription. Three nonconsecutive training days are a proposed starting schedule, with individual adjustment.

Preserve separate milestones: continuous duration, actual distance completed with breaks, continuous race walking, and time over a measured distance. GPS estimates cannot certify legal technique.

These are arithmetic examples, not beginner norms or required outcomes. Prior session duration alone does not establish distance capacity, technique quality, or symptom tolerance.

## 8. Evidence boundaries and next research decisions

- Verify a current accessible ACSM edition before relying on the historical handout in user-facing coaching.
- Obtain review of the curriculum, vigorous-intensity progression, technique guidance, and symptom handling.
- Keep healthy-adult general fitness separate from condition-specific clinical pathways.
- Select the first-release goal: weekly vigorous fitness, continuous endurance, measured 5 km, or an explicitly staged combination.
- Treat program adherence and tolerability pilots as preliminary evaluation, not proof of clinical efficacy or injury prevention.
